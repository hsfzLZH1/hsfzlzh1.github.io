Programming a viscous fluid to detect whether a computation halts

1. The question

Start a viscous fluid at rest. Fix one marked particle and a region in space. Can a finite description of an external force make that particle enter the region exactly when a given computer program eventually stops?

This family of nine manuscripts claims several ways to do so in three-dimensional flat geometry. The force encodes the machine and its finite input. The fluid motion carries out the instructions, and entry into a fixed detector reports halting.

The resulting prediction problem is undecidable: no algorithm can take every force description in the constructed class and always answer correctly whether detection will occur. This is a statement about exact mathematical trajectories over arbitrarily long times. It does not mean the fluid computes answers to undecidable problems in finite time: a machine that never halts produces no finite-time halting signal.

The family also distinguishes several experiments. Some forces repeat periodically, another becomes stationary, and others decay. Most detectors follow a particle; one manuscript instead tests the evolving velocity. These alternatives have different guarantees, so they should not be combined into one imagined construction possessing every property at once.

2. Background and essential definitions

The fluid equation

For velocity u(t,x)u(t,x)u(t,x), pressure p(t,x)p(t,x)p(t,x), viscosity ν>0\nu>0ν>0, and external body force f(t,x)f(t,x)f(t,x), the equations are

∂tu+(u⋅∇)u=−∇p+νΔu+f,∇⋅u=0.\partial_tu+(u\cdot\nabla)u=-\nabla p+\nu\Delta u+f, \qquad \nabla\cdot u=0.∂t​u+(u⋅∇)u=−∇p+νΔu+f,∇⋅u=0.

The divergence condition expresses incompressibility. A solenoidal vector field is simply a divergence-free one. The nonlinear term is the acceleration due to particles moving through a spatially varying velocity; the Laplacian represents viscous diffusion.

Several constructions use the flat unit torus T3=(R/Z)3\mathbb T^3=(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z)^3T3=(R/Z)3: a cube with opposite faces identified. Others use R3\mathbb R^3R3 or R2×T\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb TR2×T. All begin with u(0,x)=0u(0,x)=0u(0,x)=0, and viscosity is fixed before the machine is supplied.

A particle with initial label aaa follows

X˙(t)=u(t,X(t)),X(0)=a.\dot X(t)=u(t,X(t)),\qquad X(0)=a.X˙(t)=u(t,X(t)),X(0)=a.

The event “X(t)∈OX(t)\in OX(t)∈O for some finite ttt” is a material-particle observation. An Eulerian observation instead inspects the velocity at locations in space, without following one label.

What counts as a finite program for a force?

A Turing machine has finitely many control states and instructions but an unbounded tape. A finite input uses only finitely many nonblank cells initially. Its halting problem asks whether its instructions eventually reach a designated stop state.

An effective smooth force is described by a finite program that evaluates the force and each requested derivative to arbitrary rational accuracy at computably specified arguments. The program also supplies the relevant derivative bounds. No efficiency bound is claimed.

Crucially, the construction installs all local instruction branches. It does not first discover the machine’s future execution and then replay a precomputed particle path. Unqualified effectiveness assumes a positive computable viscosity; for an arbitrary fixed real viscosity, evaluation is relative to access to that parameter.

Infinite information in bounded coordinates

A tape can be represented by a positional expansion. In base BBB, a word a0a1…a_0a_1\ldotsa0​a1​… can be encoded as

z=∑j≥0d(aj)B−j−1,z=\sum_{j\geq0}d(a_j)B^{-j-1},z=j≥0∑​d(aj​)B−j−1,

where the digits d(aj)d(a_j)d(aj​) represent symbols. Choosing separated digits leaves gaps between the intervals identifying different first symbols. Removing a first symbol becomes an affine expansion; inserting one becomes an affine contraction.

The unbounded tape is stored in arbitrarily fine digits of an exact real coordinate. This explains both the computational capacity and the absence of a uniform finite-precision guarantee.

3. Development history and previous partial results

Turing’s undecidability theorem supplies the logical obstruction. Moore’s work in 1990–1991 connected symbolic computation with generalized shifts, piecewise affine maps, and smooth dynamical systems. It established a route from local tape instructions to continuous trajectories.

There is an obstacle to using fluid trajectories: a smooth flow is invertible between finite times, while an ordinary instruction can erase information. Landauer discussed retaining intermediate information, and Bennett’s 1973 reversible simulation explicitly recorded instruction history. The present manuscripts use related retention principles, with several different placements of the record.

Fluid computation itself also has substantial predecessors. Cardona, Miranda, Peralta-Salas and Presas constructed Turing-complete stationary Euler flows on a three-sphere with an adapted Riemannian metric. Cardona, Miranda and Peralta-Salas obtained universal stationary Beltrami fields in Euclidean three-space; those universal fields have infinite energy and a noncompact computational set. Their work also gives robust tape-bounded simulations on the flat torus, including a viscous realization from nonzero initial velocity.

Dyhr, González-Prieto, Miranda and Peralta-Salas constructed stationary unforced Navier–Stokes computation with an adapted metric and Hodge viscosity. In that setting a harmonic velocity makes the viscous term vanish. These are important results, but the choices of geometry, initial data and observation differ from the fixed flat, externally forced, initially resting experiments here.

For velocity observations, earlier work by Cardona and collaborators encoded computation in the Euler evolution on a constructed higher-dimensional compact manifold. M7 changes the setting to positive viscosity in three flat dimensions and supplies pointwise or integral detectors.

The claimed advance is therefore a collection of explicit forced-fluid realizations under carefully separated constraints, rather than the first connection between computation and fluid dynamics.

4. What the new manuscripts claim

A common decision statement

Each particle construction fixes its geometry, viscosity, initial velocity, label aaa, and detector OOO before receiving a machine MMM and word www. It constructs a force with a unique global smooth solution in its declared comparison class such that

(∃t<∞: X(t,a)∈O)⟺M halts on w.\bigl(\exists t<\infty:\ X(t,a)\in O\bigr) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad M\text{ halts on }w.(∃t<∞: X(t,a)∈O)⟺M halts on w.

The fixed label and detector may differ between constructions. Uniqueness on the torus is among classical solutions on every finite time interval. Whole-space statements specify additional Sobolev and pressure conditions; they do not assert uniqueness without a declared solution class.

5. Why the advance matters

Importance: 4/5. If correct, this is a broad constructive contribution linking computability, incompressible geometry and dissipative PDEs. Its significance lies in explicit realizations with fixed flat geometry, zero initial velocity, and several sharply distinguished restrictions on the force and observer.

Smoothness and uniqueness do not guarantee that every long-term event is algorithmically decidable. A perfectly smooth, explicitly described solution can carry a decision problem whose answer is not uniformly computable over all descriptions.

The decaying constructions make this especially striking. A particle may travel only a finite total distance while carrying an unbounded computation in finer spatial scales. The exact observer is essential to that conclusion. The work does not establish a robust physical computer with unlimited storage at fixed measurement accuracy.

6. The main idea

Preserve information before moving fluid

Two tape halves occupy two positional coordinates. An instruction reads finitely many symbols, so it acts on an entire rectangle of codes sharing those prefixes. For reciprocal maps, expansion in one direction is balanced by contraction in the other.

Information that would be overwritten must remain somewhere. M1 and M3 use finite reversible recording tables. M2 stores branch indices in a third real coordinate and reads the newest digit through a fine periodic gate. M5 explores other prefix and history arrangements. M9 uses three stacks with balanced total prefix lengths, making the corresponding three-dimensional affine map have determinant one.

The distinction between injectivity on one chosen run and injectivity on the full allowed instruction domain matters: the latter separates target blocks for arbitrary unused tails.

Route whole neighborhoods and control the journey

Even when source blocks are mutually disjoint and target blocks are mutually disjoint, a target can overlap another source. Moving one block directly may disturb information waiting elsewhere.

The geometric constructions use private heights or temporary storage, followed by delivery along separated routes. Local curls and shear pulses preserve divergence-free motion. In M9, obstacle detours transport full solid boxes, not just a zero-thickness coding surface. When a planar map changes area, a compensating normal expansion or contraction preserves three-dimensional volume.

Endpoint correctness alone is insufficient: a nonhalting particle must avoid the detector throughout every intermediate motion. The manuscripts therefore include coordinate bounds, height guards or detours appropriate to each observer.

Convert the mechanism into a forced solution

Once a smooth divergence-free velocity UUU has been prescribed, define

f=∂tU+(U⋅∇)U−νΔU.f=\partial_tU+(U\cdot\nabla)U-\nu\Delta U.f=∂t​U+(U⋅∇)U−νΔU.

Then (U,0)(U,0)(U,0) satisfies the fluid equations. The substantive construction is the effective computational velocity and its all-time detector bounds. A classical difference-energy estimate gives uniqueness for these constructed data in the specified classes.

For certain shear schedules, the active velocity is transverse to its own spatial variation, so (U⋅∇)U=0(U\cdot\nabla)U=0(U⋅∇)U=0. The resulting force is directly solenoidal with zero pressure. Other torus forces can be projected to their divergence-free part, changing the pressure while preserving velocity. This projection should not be confused with the direct shear property or assumed to preserve compact support in Euclidean space.

Slow time or shrink space

A clock τ(t)\tau(t)τ(t) replaces a recurring velocity VVV by

U(t,x)=τ′(t)V(τ(t),x).U(t,x)=\tau'(t)V(\tau(t),x).U(t,x)=τ′(t)V(τ(t),x).

It preserves the entire trajectory only if accumulated computational time remains unbounded. A logarithmic clock achieves decay of order 1/t1/t1/t while still traversing infinitely many steps.

Decay faster than every inverse power makes a nonnegative clock speed integrable, so this simple slowdown cannot drive infinitely many steps of the same recurring mechanism. M6 instead retains unit physical step lengths and shrinks the spatial displacement much faster than the cost of differentiating its selectors grows. Its three memories use moving localized curls, alternating fractional coordinates, or periodic lattice selectors. Late halting signals can be arbitrarily small.

Carry a signal through diffusion

M7 uses a velocity U=(a,w)U=(a,w)U=(a,w) independent of the third coordinate, where aaa is planar and divergence free. The vertical component satisfies

∂tw+a⋅∇w=νΔw+h.\partial_tw+a\cdot\nabla w=\nu\Delta w+h.∂t​w+a⋅∇w=νΔw+h.

The horizontal velocity and scalar source hhh are prescribed; the scalar www is solved for. Thus the force does not conceal an unknown solution in its definition.

On the torus, increasingly short stirring bursts keep fresh signals close to pure transport, while waiting intervals dissipate older signals. In the expanding construction, a cutoff moving with the computational address controls escaped scalar mass. These two methods pay for diffusion with different resources.

7. Scope, limitations, and open questions

All conclusions concern specially constructed external forces. They give no general regularity theorem or blowup result for arbitrary three-dimensional Navier–Stokes data, and no resolution of the unforced global regularity problem.

The reductions use exact real encodings and unlimited observation time. They assert neither efficient simulation nor uniform tolerance to force perturbations, measurement error, or finite-precision replacement. Even M7’s strict detection thresholds do not themselves imply stability under arbitrary perturbations of the forcing.

Periodicity, eventual stationarity and decay are separate choices. Directly solenoidal forces, zero pressure, fixed compact support and globally bounded derivatives are asserted only where the individual construction supplies them.

A natural further direction is to understand which computational behaviors persist under specified noise or finite storage constraints. The existing robust tape-bounded predecessors illustrate why that is a different question from unrestricted exact universality.
