Counting distinct values of Euler’s totient function

1. The question

Euler’s totient function φ(n)\varphi(n)φ(n) counts the integers from one to nnn that are coprime to nnn. Its values repeat: φ(5)=φ(8)=φ(10)=φ(12)=4\varphi(5)=\varphi(8)=\varphi(10)=\varphi(12)=4φ(5)=φ(8)=φ(10)=φ(12)=4. Other integers never occur. How many different outputs are at most xxx?

This is not the same as counting inputs n≤xn\le xn≤x, or even counting all inputs whose outputs are at most xxx. Every output must be counted exactly once, regardless of how many preimages it has.

Family 024 claims an explicit asymptotic equivalent for the number of distinct totients. It also answers a simpler-looking question of Erdős and Hall: if the output bound is multiplied by a fixed constant ccc, does the number of outputs asymptotically multiply by ccc too? The manuscript’s answer is yes.

For example, ℓ(4)=5\ell(4)=5ℓ(4)=5. Counting distinct outputs, counting all preimages and locating the least preimage are three different problems; the manuscript addresses the first and gives an additional result on the third.

3. Development history and previous partial results

Pillai proved that totient values have density zero among the positive integers. Erdős sharpened upper bounds using the typical factorization of p−1p-1p−1, and his work on products of two shifted primes improved the elementary lower bound.

Erdős and Hall obtained a much larger lower-order factor and asked whether V(cx)/V(x)→cV(cx)/V(x)\to cV(cx)/V(x)→c for fixed c>1c>1c>1. Their 1976 paper is the source of this dilation question. Pomerance brought the upper bound to the same logarithmic scale; Maier and Pomerance then identified the leading constant in the exponent:

V(x)=xlog⁡xexp⁡ ⁣((C0+o(1))(log⁡3x)2),C0≈0.8178146.V(x)=\frac{x}{\log x} \exp\!\left((C_0+o(1))(\log_3x)^2\right), \qquad C_0\approx0.8178146.V(x)=logxx​exp((C0​+o(1))(log3​x)2),C0​≈0.8178146.

Ford’s 1998 work went much further, determining the count up to a bounded multiplicative factor and describing the prime-factor structure of typical preimages. The current manuscript uses the revised 2013 version of that paper as its structural input.

Ford also proved that a fixed multiplicative interval (x,cx](x,cx](x,cx] contains a number of totients comparable to V(x)V(x)V(x). This guarantees many outputs in that interval, but does not specify the limiting ratio or remove the bounded factor in the total count.

The remaining task is delicate because multiple prime factorizations can produce the same output. Approximating every factorization by a continuous volume can overcount. The new manuscript retains the short arithmetic tail exactly and controls collisions between the long prefixes before claiming an asymptotic for distinct values.

5. Why the advance matters

Provisional importance: 3/5. Taken as stated, the theorem closes a longstanding gap between order-of-magnitude estimates and an asymptotic equivalent for the range of a fundamental arithmetic function. It also answers the Erdős–Hall fixed-dilation question.

The main term retains genuinely discrete arithmetic information. A continuous approximation captures most prime-factor geometry, but it cannot simply erase collisions among the smaller factors. The coefficient records these collisions through a finite union before taking a limit.

This is a substantial advance in the distribution of arithmetic-function values. Its scope is precise: it counts distinct totients and locates certain least preimages, without resolving all multiplicity or inverse-totient questions.

6. The main idea

Separate a long prime prefix from a short exact tail

Order the prime factors of a suitable preimage from largest to smallest. Ford’s structural estimates place their double logarithms in a region resembling a simplex, a higher-dimensional generalization of a triangle.

The manuscript splits this list twice. A long prefix is treated continuously by volume. A shorter collection of smaller primes, together with a smooth cofactor whose prime factors are bounded, is kept as exact arithmetic data. The two cuts are separated so the discarded part is small enough to absorb the tail’s complexity.

For a fixed tail cutoff HHH, the retained tail possibilities are finite and independent of xxx. The proof first lets xxx grow with HHH fixed, and only then lets HHH grow. Reversing that order would lose the finite-arithmetic interpretation.

Count outputs rather than descriptions

Different long prefixes might yield the same totient. The paper compares equations between products of shifted primes and shows that the total contribution of such prefix collisions is negligible. It uses a layered method descended from Maier–Pomerance and Ford; each sieve application involves only two or three linear forms even though the number of layers grows.

The structural coverage must also be strong enough for the least-preimage analysis: outside a negligible exceptional set, every relevant preimage has the required form. Finding one convenient preimage would not by itself control the least one.

After prefix collisions have been removed, different exact tails can still give the same value. They are deliberately not declared unique. Instead, all regions permitted by tails with the same totient are combined by taking their union. Inclusion–exclusion counts that union once.

7. Scope, limitations, and open questions

The coefficient is explicit through finite arithmetic approximants and a uniform limit. The theorem does not assert that it is constant, continuous or easy to evaluate efficiently. No numerical convergence rate for computing it is part of the headline statement.

The dilation factor ccc is fixed. The result does not automatically give uniform estimates for ccc depending on xxx, or asymptotics in short additive intervals.

Most importantly, uniqueness of the long prefix is not uniqueness of the whole preimage. Large fibers of φ\varphiφ, including those discussed in family 011, are compatible with this theorem and are not an input to it.

The question whether ℓ(d)/d\ell(d)/dℓ(d)/d is unbounded remains unresolved here. Equivalently, the least-preimage positive alternative is not established for every integer kkk. An asymptotic formula for the range does not settle all inverse problems for the function.
