From local infinite size to Cuntz-algebra absorption

2026-10-25

1. The question

Infinite-dimensional objects can sometimes contain two copies of themselves. For positive operators in a C*-algebra, Cuntz comparison makes this idea precise: an element is properly infinite if two copies can be fitted into one in the comparison sense.

Several versions of pure infiniteness ask whether this happens for every positive element, only after a fixed matrix amplification, or with simultaneous control of interactions between two elements. In nonsimple algebras, these requirements were not interchangeable by definition.

Family 303 claims that ordinary pure infiniteness already implies the strong simultaneous version for every complex C*-algebra. Under exactness, it also removes a fixed finite amplification. For separable nuclear algebras, established absorption theorems then identify all these conditions with tensorial absorption of the Cuntz algebra O∞\mathcal O_\inftyO∞​.

2. Background and essential definitions

Let CCC be a complex C*-algebra, possibly nonunital. For positive elements in its stabilization C⊗KC\otimes\mathcal KC⊗K, write a≾ba\precsim ba≾b if some sequence satisfies vj∗bvj→av_j^*bv_j\to avj∗​bvj​→a in norm. Here K\mathcal KK is the compact operators, allowing different matrix sizes to be compared by adding zero corners.

A positive element hhh is properly infinite if

h⊕h≾h.h\oplus h\precsim h.h⊕h≾h.

The symbol ⊕\oplus⊕ denotes a block-diagonal direct sum. The convention includes zero as properly infinite. Ordinary pure infiniteness in the Kirchberg–Rørdam nonsimple sense is equivalent to requiring this for every positive element.

The fixed-amplification form of weak pure infiniteness requires one integer n≥1n\geq1n≥1 such that h⊕nh^{\oplus n}h⊕n is properly infinite for every h∈C+h\in C_+h∈C+​. The integer is chosen before hhh; allowing a different uncontrolled integer for each element would change the hypothesis.

Strong pure infiniteness demands compatible comparison for pairs. One formulation says that for positive x,yx,yx,y and every ε>0\varepsilon>0ε>0, there are s,t∈Cs,t\in Cs,t∈C with

∥s∗x2s−x2∥<ε,∥t∗y2t−y2∥<ε,\|s^*x^2s-x^2\|<\varepsilon,\qquad \|t^*y^2t-y^2\|<\varepsilon,∥s∗x2s−x2∥<ε,∥t∗y2t−y2∥<ε,

and

∥s∗xyt∥<ε.\|s^*xyt\|<\varepsilon.∥s∗xyt∥<ε.

The first two inequalities retain the diagonal information, while the third suppresses the mixed interaction. No norm bound on s,ts,ts,t is part of this definition.

An algebra is exact if spatial tensoring preserves short exact sequences. Nuclearity, the existence of completely positive finite-dimensional approximations, implies exactness. The algebra O∞\mathcal O_\inftyO∞​ is generated by countably many isometries with mutually orthogonal ranges. Absorption means A≅A⊗min⁡O∞A\cong A\otimes_{\min}\mathcal O_\inftyA≅A⊗min​O∞​.

3. Development history and previous partial results

Kirchberg and Rørdam developed weak, ordinary and strong pure infiniteness to extend the simple-algebra theory to algebras with ideals. Strong pure infiniteness naturally supports O∞\mathcal O_\inftyO∞​-absorption, but an individual positive-element comparison need not visibly control mixed entries.

Several structural classes were already understood. Kirchberg–Rørdam treated simple, real-rank-zero and approximately divisible cases. Blanchard–Kirchberg obtained ordinary-to-strong pure infiniteness when the primitive ideal space is Hausdorff. Rørdam obtained the same implication for Jiang–Su-absorbing purely infinite algebras.

Other results used ideal structure. Pasnicu–Rørdam proved equivalence of the notions when projections separate ideals. Work on topological dimension zero, where the primitive ideal space has a basis of compact open sets, gave additional ordinary-to-strong and weak-to-ordinary implications.

The weak-to-ordinary direction is connected to the Global Glimm Property, which produces suitably large square-zero elements inside hereditary subalgebras. Such an element supplies orthogonal equivalent source and range pieces. Earlier results show that enough of this halving structure permits removal of a fixed amplification.

Finally, Kirchberg–Rørdam established the relationship between strong pure infiniteness and O∞\mathcal O_\inftyO∞​-absorption in stable or projection-approximate-unit settings. Kirchberg’s later central-sequence results cover general nonunital separable nuclear algebras. M1 uses that established absorption bridge after proving its new comparison implications.

4. What the new manuscripts claim

Ordinary implies strong without additional regularity

Suppose every positive element of a complex C*-algebra CCC is properly infinite. M1 claims that for all positive a,ba,ba,b, every c∈Cc\in Cc∈C, and every ε>0\varepsilon>0ε>0, there exist s,t∈Cs,t\in Cs,t∈C such that

∥s∗as−a∥<ε,∥t∗bt−b∥<ε,∥s∗ct∥<ε.\|s^*as-a\|<\varepsilon,\qquad \|t^*bt-b\|<\varepsilon,\qquad \|s^*ct\|<\varepsilon.∥s∗as−a∥<ε,∥t∗bt−b∥<ε,∥s∗ct∥<ε.

The cross term ccc is arbitrary, not necessarily a product of the diagonal inputs. Choosing (a,b,c)=(x2,y2,xy)(a,b,c)=(x^2,y^2,xy)(a,b,c)=(x2,y2,xy) gives strong pure infiniteness. This theorem assumes neither exactness, nuclearity, separability, unitality nor a restriction on the ideal space.

Exactness removes one fixed amplification

If CCC is exact and one integer nnn makes every h⊕nh^{\oplus n}h⊕n properly infinite, M1 claims that every hhh itself is properly infinite. The first theorem then makes CCC strongly purely infinite.

Thus the three notions agree for exact algebras, including nonseparable and nonunital ones. The paper also supplies an independent nuclear argument, while explaining how quotient exactness extends that route further. Its direct exact proof uses a different quantitative construction.

Nuclear absorption

For every separable nuclear complex C*-algebra AAA, the fixed-amplification condition therefore implies

A≅A⊗min⁡O∞.A\cong A\otimes_{\min}\mathcal O_\infty.A≅A⊗min​O∞​.

Together with established reverse implications, M1 identifies weak pure infiniteness, ordinary pure infiniteness, strong pure infiniteness and O∞\mathcal O_\inftyO∞​-absorption in this class. No simplicity or unit is required.

The weak-to-ordinary implication for arbitrary nonexact algebras is explicitly left outside the conclusions. The general ordinary-to-strong theorem must not be read as removing the exactness assumption from the other direction.

5. Why the advance matters

Importance: 5/5. If correct, these results remove major distinctions among notions of infiniteness in broad nonsimple settings. A local condition on each positive element would already guarantee the compatibility needed to suppress arbitrary mixed entries.

For separable nuclear algebras, a single finite amplification test would characterize a global tensorial regularity property. This connects positive-element comparison with the structure of the whole algebra while allowing arbitrary ideals and nonunital examples.

The scope includes algebras with no nonzero projections. For example, the cone C0((0,1])⊗O∞C_0((0,1])\otimes\mathcal O_\inftyC0​((0,1])⊗O∞​ absorbs O∞\mathcal O_\inftyO∞​, yet any continuous projection-valued function vanishing at the missing endpoint must be zero. Positive-element comparison therefore reaches beyond reasoning only with projections or visible isometries in a unitized picture.

6. The main idea

Separate two hereditary corners

For positive hhh, the hereditary subalgebra Her⁡(h)=hCh‾\operatorname{Her}(h)=\overline{hCh}Her(h)=hCh is the part supported by hhh. The ideal generated by hhh records where it remains visible in irreducible representations.

M1 first seeks positive contractions in the hereditary subalgebras of aaa and bbb whose support projections make the mixed term a strict contraction. At the same time, those contractions must retain a definite norm in every representation detecting the relevant inputs. Otherwise the mixed term could become small simply because important diagonal information had been discarded.

A graph construction inside several orthogonal copies introduces freely chosen algebra entries. If the required separation failed in a detecting representation, equality cases would force those entries to equal predetermined measurements. Under ordinary pure infiniteness, four carefully chosen entries contradict those forced identities through an isometry with a nonzero defect.

Iterate a uniform strict improvement

The first separation only reduces the mixed compression below one. A bounded-product argument gives a uniform contraction factor, and iteration makes the compression arbitrarily small while retaining the represented norm signals.

High spectral cutoffs of the original positive elements are then compared into the separated corners. The order of choices controls the coefficients independently of the final mixed-entry accuracy. This produces simultaneous diagonal recovery and off-diagonal suppression.

Build enough orthogonal copies under exactness

Removing a fixed amplification requires a different step: constructing enough equivalent orthogonal pieces in a scalar hereditary subalgebra before using the amplified comparison.

The exact argument uses a quantitative obstruction coming from a nonexact universal algebra. Finite matrix tests force a positive error threshold for exact targets. This obstruction helps construct square-zero elements retaining a nonzero image throughout a prescribed slice of primitive quotients.

These slices are determined by a lower bound on the norm of a positive element in the quotient. They are quasi-compact even when the primitive ideal space is not Hausdorff. Repeated halving on the same slice creates sufficiently many copies to remove the fixed amplification while preserving the necessary ideal support.

A separate nuclear route

The nuclear argument uses finite tuples witnessing nonexactness as an obstruction to failure of the graph construction. A hypothetical persistent failure would place their limiting matrix norms inside a quotient of a nuclear algebra, contradicting exactness there.

Representation-dependent hereditary witnesses are then merged into one element retaining all the required norm signals. A finite ideal argument covers each spectral cutoff, and comparison returns two copies of that cutoff to the original element. This supplies ordinary pure infiniteness independently of the direct exact route.

7. Scope, limitations, and open questions

The fixed integer in weak pure infiniteness is uniform over all scalar positive elements and their spectral cutoffs. The hypothesis is not merely that some matrix amplification of the algebra contains a properly infinite unit or that selected generators have the property.

Ordinary-to-strong pure infiniteness is claimed in complete generality, but amplification removal requires exactness. The unrestricted nonexact weak-to-ordinary question remains open within the source’s stated conclusions. The absorption equivalence additionally requires separability and nuclearity.

The mixed-entry theorem permits arbitrary ccc, and its definition does not impose fixed coefficient bounds. Quantitative bounds obtained during the proof depend on the specified cutoff data; they should not be replaced by an unstated universal norm bound for every input.

The paper develops two routes for amplification removal, with different external inputs. Their existence does not weaken the assumptions of the main statements. The digest reports those comparison and absorption claims without auditing the constructions.
