1. The question

Imagine assigning a complex number to every geometric object, then sorting the objects by the angle of that number. One would like every complicated object to split into a finite sequence of simpler pieces, ordered by angle. The number must also faithfully measure the size of those pieces: arbitrarily large numerical objects should not acquire charges arbitrarily close to zero.

This is the outline of a Bridgeland stability condition. It turns a derived category, a setting containing sheaves and complexes of sheaves, into a space where one can discuss stable constituents, their moduli, and changes of stability.

Family 055 asks for particular stability conditions predicted by geometry and physics. Its first manuscript claims the prescribed symmetry at the Gepner point for every smooth quintic threefold. The second claims exact large-volume charges on every smooth projective threefold with trivial canonical bundle, and separately proves uniform numerical inequalities on arbitrary smooth projective threefolds. Their subject is more specific than the existence of some stability condition.

2. Background and essential definitions

Charges, phases, and support

A coherent sheaf generalizes a vector bundle by allowing fibers of varying dimension and singular behavior. The derived category Db(Coh⁡X)D^b(\operatorname{Coh}X)Db(CohX) also contains finite complexes of sheaves, identifying complexes with the same cohomological information. Its shift operation [1][1][1] moves a complex by one degree.

The numerical Grothendieck group Knum(X)K_{\mathrm{num}}(X)Knum​(X) records additive classes of these objects, identifying classes that have the same Euler pairings with all others. The Euler pairing is the alternating sum of dimensions of the extension groups. A central charge is a homomorphism

Z:Knum(X)⟶C.Z:K_{\mathrm{num}}(X)\longrightarrow\mathbb C.Z:Knum​(X)⟶C.

A nonzero semistable object of phase ϕ\phiϕ has Z(E)=meiπϕZ(E)=m e^{i\pi\phi}Z(E)=meiπϕ with m>0m>0m>0. Phases are real numbers, not angles modulo a full turn: shifting by [1][1][1] raises the phase by one. A stability condition specifies the semistable objects and requires every object to have a finite filtration with decreasing phases, called a Harder–Narasimhan filtration.

Its support property is a bound

∥[E]∥≤C∣Z(E)∣\|[E]\|\le C|Z(E)|∥[E]∥≤C∣Z(E)∣

for every semistable object, using a norm on the numerical vector space. It prevents numerical complexity from becoming invisible to the charge. Local finiteness imposes a corresponding finite-length condition in short phase intervals.

Hearts and tilting

A heart is an abelian category inside the derived category where kernels, quotients, and short exact sequences can be used. Coherent sheaves form one heart. A tilt constructs another by shifting a selected class of sheaves and closing under extensions. On threefolds, two successive tilts provide the standard route to geometric stability conditions.

For a sheaf, its Chern character ch⁡(E)\operatorname{ch}(E)ch(E) packages rank, divisor, curve, and point data. A real divisor class BBB gives the twisted character ch⁡B(E)=e−Bch⁡(E)\operatorname{ch}^B(E)=e^{-B}\operatorname{ch}(E)chB(E)=e−Bch(E). An ample class HHH specifies a polarization, and t>0t>0t>0 scales it. In the large-volume problem, ttt becomes large while the geometry of XXX remains fixed.

3. Development history and previous partial results

Douglas’s proposal of stability for D-branes motivated the categorical viewpoint, and Bridgeland supplied its mathematical axioms and deformation theory. Bayer, Macrì and Toda developed the double-tilt approach to threefolds, identifying inequalities involving the third Chern character as a central obstacle. Bridgeland, Bayer–Macrì–Toda.

For a quintic, Toda formulated the desired Gepner symmetry and proposed the relevant pair of tilts. The symmetry functor and its fivefold periodicity were already known. The difficult part was to construct the semistable objects with finite filtrations and support for the prescribed, generally irrational, charge. Li established the existence of numerical stability conditions on every smooth quintic in 2019. Xu subsequently proved a stronger sheaf inequality that M1 uses as an input. Thus M1’s claimed addition is the exact Gepner symmetry, not the first stability condition on a quintic. Toda’s formulation, Li’s theorem, Xu’s inequality.

For large volume, Bayer’s polynomial stability describes asymptotic phase order. A genuine Bridgeland condition at each sufficiently large finite value of ttt requires additional control. The threefold inequalities were established in important cases such as abelian threefolds, but an uncorrected inequality at every parameter is false: Schmidt and Martinez–Schmidt produced counterexamples. Corrections depending on the geometry became part of the problem. Bayer’s polynomial stability, Schmidt’s counterexample.

The historical position changed further in 2026. Li and collaborators developed general constructions; Cheng established full numerical support, while Cheng–Feyzbakhsh identified threefold hearts and proved a weak form of the BMT conjecture. M2 explicitly builds on these results. It claims prescribed ordinary and Todd-corrected charges, a common threshold over a real parameter sector, and stronger uniform inequalities. It does not claim that general nonemptiness was still open. Cheng, Cheng–Feyzbakhsh.

5. Why the advance matters

Importance: 5/5. The claims would connect two distinguished regions of threefold stability theory with explicit constructions: a highly symmetric point on every quintic and prescribed large-volume charges on all threefolds with trivial canonical bundle. They also address a uniform numerical inequality on arbitrary polarized threefolds.

Exactness matters. A nearby charge, or a charge containing an extra curve-class correction, need not represent the same geometric or physical specification. Likewise, a condition that controls only four intersection numbers may miss numerical directions when the Picard rank is greater than one. Full support and one threshold over an open parameter sector substantially strengthen a collection of isolated existence statements.

The Gepner theorem would resolve a specific longstanding symmetry conjecture. The large-volume theorem would offer a consistent starting region for studying stable complexes and their variation. Neither manuscript claims that all paths between these regions or all resulting moduli spaces have thereby been determined.

6. The main idea

Build support before completing the slicing

M1 begins with Toda’s two tilts, but varies the second cut in two independent numerical directions. Xu’s inequality supplies room for these perturbations. Combining the perturbed hearts with the symmetry Φ\PhiΦ produces a periodic grid of categories whose charges lie in narrow angular sectors.

Testing an object against all perturbations controls its rank and first Chern coordinate. Together with the complex charge, these control the remaining two coordinates of the quintic’s rank-four numerical lattice. This yields support before the final semistable filtration is constructed.

The distinction between a lattice and its image in C\mathbb CC is crucial. The charge image need not be discrete. The full numerical lattice is discrete, however, so a bound on numerical classes gives finite refinement. The source then merges shared boundary phases and establishes the slicing and local finiteness.

Preserve all numerical directions at large volume

M2 embeds the threefold in a product of projective spaces using sufficiently many line bundles. This retains the full numerical class rather than just the degrees along one polarization. It constructs ambient stability conditions with a support estimate after rescaling each Chern-character coordinate by the appropriate power of ttt.

In those scaled coordinates, fixed higher-degree corrections become small at large volume. Deformation and restriction can therefore produce the exact desired charge, with the unwanted Todd factors canceled beforehand. Surface restrictions identify the ordinary charge’s heart as the standard double tilt; the boundary at zero slope receives a separate argument.

The numerical inequality follows by another route. Restriction to surfaces and cohomological estimates give a rank-uniform error bound at one fixed volume. Reduction to positive characteristic and Frobenius pullback remove object-dependent errors in a limit. Moving along stability walls then yields the corrected all-volume inequality and an uncorrected large-volume tail. The manuscript treats this argument as independent of the categorical construction.

7. Scope, limitations, and open questions

The Gepner statement concerns smooth quintic threefolds, not arbitrary Calabi–Yau varieties or arbitrary hypersurface degrees. Its precise functor order, phase shift, and point-charge normalization belong to the theorem.

The prescribed-charge theorem requires trivial KXK_XKX​, whereas the separate inequality allows arbitrary smooth projective threefolds. Its common threshold is local in the real twist and ample-direction parameters; it is not a single bound for the entire ample cone of every variety.

Known counterexamples to the uncorrected BMT inequality remain relevant. M2 allows a correction at small volume and removes it only past a threshold. Tensoring known counterexamples can move the twist without moving them into arbitrarily large volume, so twist uniformity and volume uniformity are different issues.

Natural further questions concern the global stability manifold, paths connecting distinguished regions, wall-crossing of particular classes, and effective thresholds. Existence with a specified charge does not automatically classify stable objects or prove every predicted enumerative correspondence.
