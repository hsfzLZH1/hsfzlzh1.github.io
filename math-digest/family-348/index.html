Einstein four-manifolds from positive curvature to a topological gap

1. The question

Curvature can be measured in different ways. Sectional curvature describes a two-dimensional tangent plane. Ricci curvature averages these plane curvatures through a given direction. An Einstein metric makes that average the same in every unit direction, but it need not make all individual plane curvatures equal.

In dimension four, does adding positive sectional curvature force one of the familiar symmetric metrics? What happens at the boundary, when sectional curvature is merely nonnegative and one plane has zero curvature? And if the Einstein equation holds only approximately in an integral sense, does the underlying smooth manifold still come from a short list?

Family 348 addresses these three questions with three related manuscripts. The first classifies the zero-plane boundary case, the second derives an integral topological gap using that classification as an explicit premise, and the third treats strictly positive sectional curvature.

Their conclusions have different strengths. The exact Einstein results identify metrics up to isometry and scale. The small-error result identifies only the smooth manifold, not the original metric.

2. Background and essential definitions

Einstein curvature and its error

A Riemannian metric ggg is Einstein if

Ric⁡g=λg\operatorname{Ric}_g=\lambda gRicg​=λg

for a constant λ\lambdaλ. In real dimension four, scalar curvature is the trace of Ricci curvature, so it equals 4λ4\lambda4λ. Positive Einstein constant can be normalized by scaling to Ric⁡=3g\operatorname{Ric}=3gRic=3g, hence scalar curvature twelve.

For an arbitrary four-dimensional metric, the trace-free Ricci tensor is

Eg=Ric⁡g−Scal⁡g4g.E_g=\operatorname{Ric}_g-\frac{\operatorname{Scal}_g}{4}g.Eg​=Ricg​−4Scalg​​g.

On a connected manifold, its vanishing is equivalent to the Einstein condition. The manuscript measures the error by

D(g)=∫M∣Eg∣g2 dμg.D(g)=\int_M|E_g|_g^2\,d\mu_g.D(g)=∫M​∣Eg​∣g2​dμg​.

This integral is unchanged by constant rescaling in dimension four: squared curvature contributes the inverse square of the metric scale, while volume contributes its square. Small D(g)D(g)D(g) is therefore not achieved simply by enlarging the manifold.

Nonnegative sectional curvature is a separate assumption

For every tangent two-plane σ\sigmaσ, let Kg(σ)K_g(\sigma)Kg​(σ) be its sectional curvature. Strict positivity requires Kg(σ)>0K_g(\sigma)>0Kg​(σ)>0; nonnegativity permits zero. Neither condition should be replaced by a statement only about scalar or Ricci curvature.

A product of two round spheres illustrates the boundary. Planes tangent to one factor have positive curvature, while planes using one direction from each factor have zero curvature. With equal radii, the product metric is Einstein.

Metrics, covers, and smooth types

A universal Riemannian cover is the simply connected covering manifold equipped with the lifted metric. Identifying this cover does not necessarily identify the original quotient globally.

An isometry preserves the metric. A diffeomorphism identifies only the smooth manifold. Thus a theorem saying that MMM is diffeomorphic to S4S^4S4 does not say that its given metric is round.

The standard models here are the round four-sphere, the complex projective plane CP2\mathbb{CP}^2CP2 with its Fubini–Study metric, and the product S2×S2S^2\times S^2S2×S2 with equal round factors. Real projective four-space RP4\mathbb{RP}^4RP4 appears as a nonorientable quotient in the strictly positive case.

3. Development history and previous partial results

The Einstein classification problem in positive sectional curvature has long been studied through additional pinching conditions. Berger’s early quarter-pinching rigidity was followed by quantitative curvature criteria of Yang, Costa, and Cao–Tran. Those conditions control how far sectional curvatures can vary relative to one another or to the Einstein constant.

Gursky–LeBrun obtained the Fubini–Study conclusion under nonnegative sectional curvature together with a nonzero positive-definite intersection form. Their analysis of the two Weyl curvature components supplies an important predecessor for this family. Results under nonnegative isotropic curvature, due to Micallef–Wang and later Brendle in higher dimensions, use a different curvature condition and should not be treated as the same theorem. Gursky–LeBrun.

Further classifications retained topology or symmetry. Liu treated torus-invariant Einstein metrics in the nonnegative case. Cheng’s September 2026 preprint treated oriented positive-sectional metrics with Euler characteristic at most three. Gursky–Malchiodi obtained classification under a condition involving Euler characteristic χ\chiχ and signature τ\tauτ, namely 2χ−3∣τ∣≤42\chi-3|\tau|\leq42χ−3∣τ∣≤4. Cheng, Gursky–Malchiodi.

The exact classification claimed here removes those additional restrictions. The boundary paper emphasizes that estimates proved with strict positivity do not automatically extend when curvature reaches zero; their equality cases need separate treatment.

The approximate Einstein question has a different history. De Lellis–Topping’s almost-Schur inequality relates scalar-curvature variation to trace-free Ricci error. Ricci-flow smoothing, entropy and Sobolev estimates, and compactness theory provide tools for extracting regular limits from metrics without initial pointwise curvature bounds. M2 combines these ideas to turn small integral Einstein error into a smooth classification, once the exact classification is supplied. Almost-Schur lemma.

4. What the new manuscripts claim

Strictly positive sectional curvature: M3

Every connected smooth closed Einstein four-manifold with strictly positive sectional curvature is, up to positive scaling and isometry, one of

S4,CP2,RP4S^4,\qquad\mathbb{CP}^2,\qquad\mathbb{RP}^4S4,CP2,RP4

with the standard metrics specified above. No orientability assumption is imposed, and there is no further curvature-pinching or topological condition.

A single zero plane: M1

Suppose a connected smooth closed four-manifold satisfies

Ric⁡=3g,Kg≥0,\operatorname{Ric}=3g,\qquad K_g\geq0,Ric=3g,Kg​≥0,

and at least one tangent two-plane at one point has zero curvature. M1 claims that its universal Riemannian cover is isometric to

S2(1/3)×S2(1/3).S^2(1/\sqrt3)\times S^2(1/\sqrt3).S2(1/3​)×S2(1/3​).

Here S2(r)S^2(r)S2(r) is a round sphere of radius rrr. Under the general normalization Ric⁡=λg\operatorname{Ric}=\lambda gRic=λg, with λ>0\lambda>0λ>0, both radii become 1/λ1/\sqrt\lambda1/λ​.

Combining M1 with M3 gives the full universal-cover list under positive Einstein constant and nonnegative sectional curvature: round S4S^4S4, Fubini–Study CP2\mathbb{CP}^2CP2, or the equal-factor product. A zero plane at one point is enough to force the product alternative globally on the cover.

Small integral Einstein error: M2

M2 explicitly assumes the exact positive-Einstein classification supplied by M1’s corollary. Under that premise, it asserts a universal constant ϵ0>0\epsilon_0>0ϵ0​>0 such that every connected, simply connected, smooth closed four-manifold with Kg≥0K_g\geq0Kg​≥0 and

D(g)<ϵ0D(g)<\epsilon_0D(g)<ϵ0​

is diffeomorphic to the standard S4S^4S4, CP2\mathbb{CP}^2CP2, or S2×S2S^2\times S^2S2×S2, without a prescribed orientation.

There are no additional bounds on volume, diameter, curvature maximum, injectivity radius, Sobolev constants, or curvature derivatives. The conclusion is not an isometry statement and does not say that the original metric is Einstein.

The manuscripts are therefore connected by a dependency: M3 treats the positive interior, M1 treats its zero-curvature boundary and states the combined classification, and M2 uses that classification only after producing an exactly Einstein limit.

5. Why the advance matters

Importance: 5/5. If correct, the family settles a central four-dimensional Einstein classification problem and extends its topological consequences to metrics with small scale-invariant error.

The zero-plane theorem is particularly rigid. A single vanishing sectional curvature, combined with the global assumptions, determines a parallel product splitting on the universal cover. It is much stronger than requiring a curvature equality at every point.

The gap theorem is significant because its hypothesis supplies no pointwise curvature control. It claims that concentration and possible geometric collapse can be handled well enough to recover a smooth topological classification. At the same time, its modest conclusion about the original metric is essential to understanding what has actually been asserted.

The rating reflects these stated consequences, not mathematical verification of the analytic estimates or classification arguments.

6. The main idea

In four dimensions, curvature has two Weyl blocks

Choose an orientation. The Hodge star splits two-forms into self-dual and anti-self-dual three-dimensional spaces. For an Einstein metric normalized by Ric⁡=3g\operatorname{Ric}=3gRic=3g, the curvature operator has two diagonal blocks

I+T,I+U,I+T,\qquad I+U,I+T,I+U,

where TTT and UUU are symmetric trace-free operators, the two Weyl components. The Einstein equation removes the off-diagonal Ricci contribution but does not force either Weyl block to vanish.

Set x=−λmin⁡(T)x=-\lambda_{\min}(T)x=−λmin​(T) and y=−λmin⁡(U)y=-\lambda_{\min}(U)y=−λmin​(U). The smallest sectional curvature is

Kmin⁡=1−x+y2.K_{\min}=1-\frac{x+y}{2}.Kmin​=1−2x+y​.

Thus strict positivity gives x+y<2x+y<2x+y<2, while nonnegativity gives x+y≤2x+y\leq2x+y≤2. The exact spectral condition is stronger than a rough estimate using only the norms of the blocks.

Balance the blocks, then analyze equality

M3 combines separate lower bounds for the averaged squared Weyl norms with a scalar variance inequality and an upper volume deficit. If both blocks are nonzero, these estimates force their second moments to balance.

A weighted Hessian identity then couples the blocks more strongly. The Bianchi identity constrains their derivatives, and curvature commutators control parts of their second derivatives. Completing weighted squares and using explicit polynomial inequalities gives an integral expression that must be both nonnegative and nonpositive, with strictness whenever a Weyl norm varies.

The norms must therefore be constant. In the strictly positive case, the separate norm gaps contradict the possibility that both blocks remain nonzero. One block vanishes, and the remaining equality analysis identifies a parallel curvature tensor with a standard model. An orientation-cover argument gives the nonorientable RP4\mathbb{RP}^4RP4 case.

M1 develops the boundary version. The relevant quadratic form may now have a kernel, and Weyl blocks may vanish at individual points. The manuscript retains nonstrict inequalities and treats these zero sets explicitly. If both blocks are present, their normalized norms become one, each block has spectrum (2,−1,−1)(2,-1,-1)(2,−1,−1), and both are parallel. Their distinguished eigenlines yield two parallel rank-two tangent subbundles, producing the sphere product.

The finite polynomial certificates are part of the manuscripts’ stated argument. This digest does not run their arithmetic checkers or assess their proof coverage.

Obtain geometry from a small integral defect

For M2, suppose there were a sequence outside the three claimed smooth types with D(gj)→0D(g_j)\to0D(gj​)→0. Normalize average scalar curvature to twelve. Almost-Schur estimates make scalar curvature close to twelve in L2L^2L2, so Ricci curvature becomes close to 3gj3g_j3gj​ in the same norm.

Nonnegative sectional curvature bounds the full curvature tensor by a dimensional multiple of scalar curvature. The Chern–Gauss–Bonnet formula, together with simple connectivity, gives a volume lower bound. Segment averaging and the second variation of geodesic length then produce a diameter bound and initial Sobolev control.

This step prevents collapse before smoothing. It does not assume that small integral defect automatically gives a bound on the maximum curvature.

Smooth first, classify the limit afterward

Run Ricci flow, ∂tg=−2Ric⁡\partial_tg=-2\operatorname{Ric}∂t​g=−2Ric. The paper controls the high-curvature tail above a fixed threshold in L2L^2L2, propagates that control, and derives a common positive lifespan. At positive times, derivative estimates and compactness provide smooth convergence to an exactly Einstein flow.

Nonnegative sectional curvature is not assumed to be preserved along these flows. Instead, the manuscript estimates the integral of its negative part by a multiple of time. The limiting Einstein flow is a simple rescaling,

h(t)=(1−6t)h,Ric⁡h=3h.h(t)=(1-6t)h,\qquad\operatorname{Ric}_h=3h.h(t)=(1−6t)h,Rich​=3h.

Its explicit scaling lets the negative-part estimate tend to zero as time decreases, recovering nonnegative sectional curvature for hhh. The exact classification premise now applies.

Smooth convergence at positive time identifies the manifolds diffeomorphically with the classified limit, contradicting the chosen sequence. The convergence asserted in this argument is after regularization, not smooth convergence of the original metrics at time zero.

7. Scope, limitations, and open questions

The exact nonnegative-curvature classification assumes a positive Einstein constant. It should not be restated as covering every Einstein metric with nonnegative sectional curvature without accounting for the zero-constant case.

M1 classifies the universal cover; M3 gives the explicit global list under strict positivity. The gap theorem additionally assumes simple connectivity and concludes a smooth type rather than an exact metric.

The constant ϵ0\epsilon_0ϵ0​ is existential. The statement does not provide a numerical threshold, a quantitative distance from the original metric to a model, or a uniqueness theorem for a nearby Einstein metric.

Finally, M2’s exact classification premise is explicit, not an independent reproof of M1 and M3. The digest reports the three claims together while preserving that logical dependence and without checking their proofs.
