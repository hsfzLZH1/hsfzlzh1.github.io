Three forms of regularity become one: comparison, dimension and Jiang–Su absorption

1. The question

An infinite-dimensional operator algebra can be regular in several apparently different ways. Positive operators may be ordered by their numerical sizes. Finite-dimensional approximations may need only a bounded number of pieces. Tensoring with a particular infinite-dimensional algebra may leave its isomorphism type unchanged.

The Toms–Winter program asks when these properties coincide. Family 291 claims the missing comparison-to-absorption implication in broad simple settings, a dimension-and-absorption equivalence beyond simplicity, and an equivariant version for amenable group actions. A fourth manuscript develops trace-splitting constructions underlying these regularity phenomena.

The four papers have different hypotheses. In particular, the dynamical theorem assumes the original algebra is already Jiang–Su stable, and the nonsimple dimension equivalence excludes elementary ideal subquotients. Keeping those restrictions visible is essential to understanding what has been claimed.

2. Background and essential definitions

A C*-algebra is a norm-closed algebra of operators closed under adjoints. It is simple if it has no nonzero proper closed two-sided ideals, and separable if a countable set is norm dense. Nuclearity means that its identity map admits finite-dimensional completely positive approximations. Completely positive maps preserve positivity even when applied entrywise to matrices.

The Jiang–Su algebra Z\mathcal ZZ is a distinguished simple, unital, infinite-dimensional C*-algebra whose standard classification invariant resembles that of the complex numbers. An algebra is Jiang–Su stable if

A≅A⊗Z.A\cong A\otimes\mathcal Z.A≅A⊗Z.

Tensor products are spatial unless explicitly marked maximal. Absorption expresses a strong internal regularity, not finite dimensionality.

For positive elements in A⊗KA\otimes\mathcal KA⊗K, where K\mathcal KK denotes compact operators, write a≾ba\precsim ba≾b if vj∗bvj→av_j^*bv_j\to avj∗​bvj​→a in norm for some sequence. This Cuntz comparison is a flexible version of fitting one positive operator inside another. Mutual comparison gives classes forming the ordered Cuntz semigroup Cu(A)\mathrm{Cu}(A)Cu(A), with addition and increasing suprema.

A trace τ\tauτ gives a rank-like quantity

dτ(a)=lim⁡n→∞τ(a1/n),d_\tau(a)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau(a^{1/n}),dτ​(a)=n→∞lim​τ(a1/n),

using the unnormalized matrix trace after stabilization. Traces and ranks may be infinite. Functionals on Cu(A)\mathrm{Cu}(A)Cu(A) preserve addition, order and increasing suprema, taking values in [0,∞][0,\infty][0,∞].

Nuclear dimension bounds the number of orthogonality-preserving pieces in finite-dimensional approximations. Dimension at most one means two such pieces suffice. Uniform property Gamma provides approximately central partitions splitting every coefficient trace evenly, simultaneously for all traces. Equal traces of the partition pieces alone are insufficient.

3. Development history and previous partial results

Jiang and Su introduced their algebra in the classification program. Rørdam showed that its absorption forces strong comparison properties. Toms and Winter linked absorption, finite nuclear dimension and strict comparison as three manifestations of regularity.

Winter established absorption from finite nuclear dimension in the simple setting. Work culminating in Castillejos–Evington–Tikuisis–White–Winter supplied the converse dimension bound, with Castillejos–Evington treating the nonunital simple case. Thus comparison-to-absorption was the remaining direction of the simple regularity triangle addressed here.

Matui–Sato proved that direction with finitely many extremal traces. Sato, Kirchberg–Rørdam and Toms–White–Winter extended methods to suitable compact finite-dimensional trace boundaries. Later uniform-Gamma criteria isolated the missing central divisibility: strict comparison plus uniform property Gamma yields absorption. Pointwise information at each trace was not enough; one construction had to work across the whole trace space.

Outside simplicity, ideals create new obstacles. A trace can be finite on one ideal and infinite elsewhere, and finite and purely infinite behavior can coexist. Robert–Tikuisis developed a nonsimple absorption strategy and identified central splitting conditions. Prior dimension-collapse results covered several special nonsimple classes, including purely infinite absorption and locally subhomogeneous settings.

For dynamics, Szabó proposed automatic equivariant Jiang–Su stability for amenable actions on already stable algebras. Szabó–Wouters established an equivariant uniform-Gamma criterion. M1 uses that existing absorption theorem after constructing invariant trace splittings without restricting how the action moves traces.

4. What the new manuscripts claim

M2: comparison gives absorption

For a separable simple nuclear non-elementary algebra, M2 claims that strict comparison implies Jiang–Su stability, including nonunital algebras and unbounded traces. “Non-elementary” excludes algebras of compact operators on any Hilbert space.

Its precise extended-functional comparison convention says that x≤yx\leq yx≤y follows whenever every functional fff satisfies f(x)≤f(y)f(x)\leq f(y)f(x)≤f(y), with strict inequality whenever 0<f(y)<∞0<f(y)<\infty0<f(y)<∞. Zero and infinite target values therefore remain part of the convention; they are not discarded by normalizing traces.

More generally, for separable nuclear algebras, M2 obtains absorption from almost unperforation and full almost divisibility:

(ℓ+1)x≤ℓy⟹x≤y,(\ell+1)x\leq\ell y\Longrightarrow x\leq y,(ℓ+1)x≤ℓy⟹x≤y,

and, for every positive integer NNN and class xxx, existence of one class uuu satisfying

Nu≤x≤(N+1)u.Nu\leq x\leq(N+1)u.Nu≤x≤(N+1)u.

The same uuu must satisfy both inequalities. As a consequence, M2 claims

A≅A⊗Z⟺Cu(A)≅Cu(A⊗Z)A\cong A\otimes\mathcal Z \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathrm{Cu}(A)\cong\mathrm{Cu}(A\otimes\mathcal Z)A≅A⊗Z⟺Cu(A)≅Cu(A⊗Z)

for separable nuclear AAA. The right-hand isomorphism may be abstract in the Cuntz-semigroup category, preserving its full order and supremum structure; it need not come from a *-homomorphism. The canonical first-factor map is a special case.

M3: nonsimple dimension collapses to at most one

For separable nuclear AAA with no nonzero elementary ideal subquotient, M3 claims equivalence of finite nuclear dimension, Jiang–Su stability, and nuclear dimension at most one. An ideal subquotient is I/JI/JI/J for ideals J⊆I⊆AJ\subseteq I\subseteq AJ⊆I⊆A; the excluded elementary algebras are K(H)\mathcal K(H)K(H).

The broader dimension statement is

dim⁡nuc(A0⊗Z)≤1\dim_{\mathrm{nuc}}(A_0\otimes\mathcal Z)\leq1dimnuc​(A0​⊗Z)≤1

for every separable nuclear A0A_0A0​. The exclusion is needed for the reverse implication from finite dimension to absorption: a matrix algebra has dimension zero but does not absorb Z\mathcal ZZ.

M3 also gives an independent splitting theorem: a nonzero unital C*-algebra without characters has two full orthogonal positive elements in some finite maximal tensor power. A character is a nonzero *-homomorphism to C\mathbb CC, and “full” means generating the whole algebra as an ideal. This auxiliary theorem assumes neither nuclearity nor separability.

M4: three routes to central trace splitting

For simple separable unital infinite-dimensional nuclear stably finite AAA with traces, M4 claims that real rank zero of the uniform tracial ultrapower of its uniform tracial completion implies uniform property Gamma. Real rank zero means norm approximation of self-adjoint elements by finite-spectrum ones. The completion and ultrapower are specified constructions, not interchangeable with an arbitrary norm ultrapower.

Independently, M4 proves the unital strict-comparison-to-absorption implication. Its traceless case uses comparison with every nonzero positive target; absence of traces alone is not the hypothesis.

A third route treats simple separable nuclear stably projectionless algebras: every densely finite lower-semicontinuous trace must be bounded, the normalized trace base must be nonempty and compact, and comparison is tested on rays with positive finite target rank. Under these conditions M4 claims uniform Gamma and absorption. This route does not assume the real-rank-zero condition of the first theorem.

5. Why the advance matters

Importance: 5/5. If correct, these results connect numerical order, approximation complexity, tensorial regularity and dynamics across a large portion of nuclear C*-algebra theory. The comparison implication completes the simple regularity triangle under the stated conventions, while the nonsimple theorem incorporates arbitrary ideal structure subject to its elementary-subquotient exclusion.

The dimension bound is striking: after absorbing Z\mathcal ZZ, arbitrarily complicated separable nuclear input would admit two-piece approximations. This is a statement about noncommutative approximation dimension, not the ordinary dimension of a space underlying every algebra.

The dynamical result adds that regularity can coexist with arbitrary amenable symmetries in the specified finite unital setting. It does not require choosing an action that leaves each trace fixed.

6. The main idea

Convert numerical divisibility into central structure

M2 first turns comparison into division of Cuntz classes. In nonunital settings it measures rounding errors against one fixed spectral cut with controlled support rank. This accommodates unbounded traces without pretending that a compact normalized trace space always exists.

Nuclear approximations and positive maps preserving orthogonality then produce central matrix structures. Separate arguments handle visibility in ideals and largeness in traces. Packing those structures yields a copy of Z\mathcal ZZ in the appropriate central-sequence quotient, which gives absorption.

Assemble approximations across ideals and traces

M3 localizes finite-dimensional approximations in hereditary corners, rounds weights and compares positive elements with extended rank functions. Two weighted conjugacies produce the two-color dimension bound. For the reverse implication, a finite tensor splitting construction supplies full orthogonal central elements, feeding the established Robert–Tikuisis absorption criterion.

M4 works directly with projections and coefficient identities. Nuclear averaging and controlled packing make trace splittings central. Conditional central-limit arguments and bounded truncations preserve uniform estimates over all traces. Its three routes supply their own hypotheses and constructions; they are not one theorem with freely interchangeable assumptions.

Make the splitting invariant under the action

M1 places many commuting orbit algebras in a uniform tracial central-sequence algebra. Their trace distributions permit a central-limit construction. Amenability provides finite Følner sets whose boundaries are small relative to their size.

A covariance-normalized quadratic construction prevents degeneracy even when orbit variables are highly correlated. It produces almost invariant distributions uniformly across the trace space. Diagonal selections give invariant equal-trace central projections, establishing equivariant uniform Gamma. The existing Szabó–Wouters theorem supplies the final cocycle-conjugacy step.

7. Scope, limitations, and open questions

Nuclearity and separability remain central hypotheses of the main absorption and dimension results. Simple strict comparison is not the same as merely comparing a selected family of bounded traces. M2 explicitly uses the extended functional cone; M4’s projectionless theorem states its own finite-target-rank convention and compact-base assumptions.

M3’s elementary-subquotient exclusion cannot be omitted from the finite-dimension-to-absorption implication. Its abstract Cuntz-semigroup equivalences invoke M2. The independent maximal-tensor splitting theorem should not be confused with spatial tensor products throughout the main nuclear statements.

M1 assumes existing Jiang–Su stability and proves cocycle conjugacy, allowing the specified unitary correction. It does not assert literal conjugacy with no cocycle, extend to nonamenable groups, or settle the nonunital equivariant case. These boundaries remain part of the mathematical question after the claimed advances.
