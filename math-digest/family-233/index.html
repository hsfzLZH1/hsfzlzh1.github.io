1. The question

A statistical-mechanics model can have several descriptions. One records spins, another draws connected clusters, and a third builds a random height surface. Understanding each separately does not explain how they fit together. Two random objects can have familiar individual distributions while their dependence contains substantial additional information.

The Ashkin–Teller model provides a particularly rich example. Its critical parameters form a line rather than an isolated point. Along that line, a height representation is expected to approach a Gaussian free field, while two coupled collections of current clusters should become geometric structures determined by that same field.

Family 233 contains one manuscript claiming this joint continuum description throughout the stated critical line, including its four-state Potts endpoint. It retains the complete compact supports of the clusters, their nesting at every depth, and the special cluster touching the wired boundary. The proposed limit explains how a random surface encodes two interacting geometries.

2. Background and essential definitions

From spins to currents

The usual Ashkin–Teller model couples two Ising spin systems. Each vertex carries two signs, and the interaction rewards agreements within each system and an additional product of the two agreements. The dimensionless interaction strengths are denoted by JJJ and UUU.

The manuscript fixes

J>0,U≤J,sinh⁡(2J)=e−2U.J>0,\qquad U\leq J,\qquad \sinh(2J)=e^{-2U}.J>0,U≤J,sinh(2J)=e−2U.

Here ggg is a continuum coupling parameter, not a lattice spacing. The lattice spacing will be δ\deltaδ.

A current is an edge configuration obtained from an expansion of the spin model. For the theorem, it is enough to describe the equivalent joint construction. Put signs τ\tauτ on square-lattice vertices and signs τ†\tau^\daggerτ† on the centers of bounded faces. The latter form the dual lattice. Fix the primal boundary signs to +1+1+1, and leave the dual boundary free.

Across a pair of crossing primal and dual edges, the two spin systems are forbidden from disagreeing simultaneously. Give an allowed spin configuration weight proportional to ttt raised to its total number of disagreements, where t=1/ct=1/ct=1/c. If the dual signs disagree, open the primal edge and close the dual edge; do the reverse if the primal signs disagree. If neither disagrees, open only the primal edge, only the dual edge, or neither, with probabilities 1−t,1−t,2t−11-t,1-t,2t-11−t,1−t,2t−1, respectively. The primal boundary edges are open.

Thus the two open-edge sets never cross. They are not complements: both edges can be closed. Their connected components are the current clusters. The primal collection has a wired boundary cluster; the dual collection has free boundary conditions.

A two-dimensional Gaussian free field, or GFF, is a random generalized function. 3. Development history and previous partial results

Ashkin and Teller introduced their four-component lattice model in 1943. Its coupled spins later became an important setting for critical behavior that varies continuously with a parameter. The geometric representations used here developed through work relating spin systems, percolation and height functions; Lis gave the precise spin–edge–height framework and current weights on which this manuscript builds. Original model, Lis’s representation.

An essential solved case occurs at U=0U=0U=0, where g=2g=2g=2. The relevant currents are then double random currents. Duminil-Copin and Lis identified their signed nesting field with a dimer height. Duminil-Copin, Lis and Qian subsequently obtained conformally invariant limits of the height and recursive cluster-boundary families. This supplied both a concrete continuum picture and an exploration strategy. Nesting field, double-current limits.

In 2026, Alcalde López, Heeney and Lis described the joint height and complete current-cluster collections at the Ising point, and formulated the extension along the critical line addressed here. Their broader work couples Ising magnetisation fields to a GFF. A separate paper by Alcalde López and Sepúlveda studies a stronger Ising-point excursion decomposition involving additional fields and measures. Those extra conclusions should not be attributed to the present theorem. Critical-line conjecture and Ising coupling, excursion decomposition.

The height is also related to a six-vertex model with weights (1,1,c)(1,1,c)(1,1,c). Duminil-Copin, Kozlowski, Lammers and Manolescu proved a GFF limit for 3≤c≤2\sqrt3\leq c\leq23​≤c≤2, and developed estimates on a larger interval. The new manuscript uses those estimates while claiming the missing covariance and Gaussianity arguments needed for its full range. The full-plane companion described in family 225 is related, but does not supply the random-domain cluster theorem by itself. Earlier six-vertex work.

The historical gap is therefore specific: extend a known joint picture beyond the Ising point, with the appropriate varying coupling, and identify entire cluster supports even near rough boundaries.

4. What the new manuscripts claim

A limit for every admissible approximation

Let DDD be a bounded Jordan domain: its boundary is a simple closed curve, without a smoothness assumption. Approximate it by simple square-lattice polygons whose boundaries converge under parametrization and whose interiors eventually contain every compact subset of DDD.

For every fixed allowed (J,U)(J,U)(J,U), Theorem 2.3 claims the joint limit

The superscripts distinguish wired and free collections. The final component singles out the wired boundary cluster. The convergence is along the full mesh limit, with no specially chosen subsequence.

Height convergence is in local Sobolev spaces Hlocs(D)H^s_{\mathrm{loc}}(D)Hlocs​(D) for every s<−1s<-1s<−1. These spaces permit distributions rather than pointwise functions. Equivalently for the basic interpretation, integrate the lattice height against smooth functions and obtain the corresponding Gaussian averages with the stated normalization.

For clusters, every set above a fixed positive diameter must have a nearby counterpart in Hausdorff distance, in both directions. Hausdorff distance measures whether every point of either compact set lies near the other set. The matching is not required to be one-to-one, and the topology ignores multiplicities and singleton clusters. The distinguished boundary cluster is tracked separately. The continuum collections contain only finitely many clusters larger than any fixed positive size.

5. Why the advance matters

Importance: 4/5. If correct, the claim gives a broad geometric description of an interacting critical line, extending a detailed special-case theory to continuously varying parameters and the four-state Potts endpoint.

The gain over a height theorem is substantial. Knowing that a surface becomes Gaussian does not identify which microscopic edges belong to the same macroscopic object. Here, entire cluster supports and the wired boundary component are tied to canonical structures of that Gaussian field.

The result also makes parameter dependence concrete. At the Ising point, g=2g=2g=2; at the four-state Potts endpoint, g=4g=4g=4. The splitting height aaa changes accordingly, so one common family of continuum constructions describes distinct lattice interactions. The score reflects this geometric unification, while recognizing the major foundations already established in the Ising and six-vertex settings.

6. The main idea

The manuscript separates two tasks: identify the random field, then recover the complete geometry coupled to it.

First, it develops exact conditional laws for cutting along suitable explored boundaries. A current and an auxiliary complementary spin percolation are used together. This auxiliary complement must not be confused with the other current in the theorem. An appropriate explored perimeter leaves a region with constant boundary height, making its conditional field accessible.

Second, reflection positivity and estimates from the six-vertex representation constrain the plane covariance. Screening arguments control how local insertions respond to distant boundary information. Moment identities then identify Gaussian limits in the flat regions. The normalization is extracted from the free-energy response only after the necessary Gaussian convergence has been obtained.

Third, stop cluster explorations before their geometry becomes uncontrolled. The conditional limiting field in the remaining regions has the harmonic constants and independent GFF remainders characteristic of two-valued local sets. This connects discrete stopping rules to the recursive continuum constructions.

There remains a geometric problem: some pieces of a current cluster might escape detection by the height jumps around its visible holes. The manuscript uses local passage tests, collar bounds and a no-crossing argument to rule out such missing macroscopic pieces. It then matches the complete collections, including the boundary cluster and all nesting depths.

The guiding principle is that field convergence supplies information about fluctuations, while additional exploration estimates determine which fluctuations belong to the same geometric cluster. Neither step automatically replaces the other.

7. Scope, limitations, and open questions

The parameters are fixed before taking the mesh to zero. There is no stated uniformity as c↓1c\downarrow1c↓1, equivalently g↓4/3g\downarrow4/3g↓4/3. That excluded limiting value cannot simply be added by continuity.

The result uses the specified wired/free coupling on admissible square-lattice approximations of bounded Jordan domains. It does not assert every boundary condition, lattice or off-critical perturbation has the same limit.

Cluster convergence concerns compact supports in the stated noninjective matching topology. It does not assign a limiting area measure, preserve cluster multiplicities, supply natural parametrizations, or identify all spin-domain-wall curves. Nor does it give an exact metric exponent for travelling within a cluster.

The theorem includes the four-state Potts endpoint at a fixed parameter value. This should be distinguished from taking a second limit along the critical line toward that endpoint. Its normalization also belongs to the height convention used here; replacing HHH by the unit-corner-increment height 2H2H2H changes the multiplier.

Natural further problems concern geometric measures, additional spin observables and boundary conditions beyond this coupling. Some richer statements are already known at the Ising point; extending them through the full critical line would require additional conclusions beyond this manuscript.

8. Sources and further reading

The principal source is M1, especially Sections 2.1–2.7 for the model, recursive sets, topology and joint theorem. Its introduction explains the relation to prior double-current and six-vertex results.

For the continuum objects, Aru, Sepúlveda and Werner develop bounded-type thin local sets, while Aru and Sepúlveda study two-valued sets and their geometry. These give the conditional-GFF meaning behind the recursive description. Family 232 offers a related introduction to height limits and the distinction between a field theorem and an interface theorem.
