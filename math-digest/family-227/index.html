The dynamical phase transition in the Sherrington–Kirkpatrick model

hsfzLZH1
2026-10-08
Math Digest
Markov chains, Probability, Statistical mechanics
← All digests

Family 227 · Probability and statistical mechanics
Importance 4/5 · Provisional editorial rating
Sources consulted 2026-10-08

A system can have a perfectly well-defined equilibrium distribution and still take an enormous time to reach it. Family 227 studies this gap in a central model of disordered magnetism. Its eight manuscripts claim a transition from logarithmic relaxation time to a critical power law and then to much slower relaxation. Understanding the claim requires care about both the clock and the initial state.

1. The question

Imagine many tiny magnets, each pointing either up or down. Some pairs prefer to agree; others prefer to disagree. These preferences can conflict: three magnets cannot all disagree with each other. Such frustration produces competing configurations rather than a single obvious arrangement.

Allow one magnet at a time to reconsider its direction, with some thermal randomness. How long until the distribution of configurations forgets where it started?

This is a question about mixing time. Knowing which configurations are likely at equilibrium does not tell us how quickly local updates explore them. Family 227 asks how that exploration changes as temperature crosses the spin-glass transition. The precise results discussed below are claims of the linked manuscripts; their scope and limitations are discussed in Section 7.

2. Background and essential definitions

Spins and random interactions

A configuration is a vector σ=(σ1,…,σn)∈{−1,+1}n\sigma=(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_n)\in\{-1,+1\}^nσ=(σ1​,…,σn​)∈{−1,+1}n. For each pair i<ji<ji<j, draw an independent standard Gaussian variable JijJ_{ij}Jij​, meaning a normal variable of mean zero and variance one. These couplings are drawn once and held fixed. 3. Development history and previous partial results

From equilibrium to dynamics

Sherrington and Kirkpatrick introduced their model in 1975 to study magnetism with competing random interactions. Their original paper concerns the equilibrium phases of an infinite-range model.

Parisi’s later description of equilibrium led to a formula for the limiting free energy, a quantity derived from log⁡Z\log ZlogZ. Guerra’s interpolation bound and Talagrand’s 2006 proof established that formula rigorously. This was a major equilibrium achievement, but a formula for the weights of configurations does not specify how quickly a local dynamics moves among them.

For critical dynamics, numerical work already pointed toward the scale n2/3n^{2/3}n2/3. Billoire and Campbell’s 2011 study, especially its finite-size scaling analysis, examined equilibrium spin autocorrelations. Such evidence suggests a relaxation scale; it does not establish a bound for every initial configuration.

Rigorous progress before this family

High-temperature results gradually extended the range of provably efficient local sampling. Eldan, Koehler, and Zeitouni obtained a spectral criterion giving an SK result for β<1/4\beta<1/4β<1/4. Later localization methods improved that range. In August 2026, Wang obtained optimal-order O(nlog⁡n)O(n\log n)O(nlogn) random-site mixing for every fixed β<1/2\beta<1/2β<1/2, including external fields. In September 2026, Boban, Li, and Oveis Gharan obtained polynomial mixing a fixed distance beyond 1/21/21/2. These advances still left a gap before the zero-field threshold 111.

The low-temperature story also depends on the starting state. Sellke’s 2025 preprint established exponential worst-case mixing at sufficiently low temperature. It explained why a physics prediction of stretched-exponential relaxation should not be interpreted as a worst-case bound. Rare, exceptionally slow configurations can dominate a maximum without describing an equilibrium sample.

Family 227 claims to close the high-temperature gap, identify the critical mixing exponent, and bound low-temperature relaxation for typical fixed equilibrium states. These are distinct advances with different quantifiers.

High temperature and cutoff

The spectral-gap manuscript claims a positive relaxation rate independent of nnn for each fixed 0<β<10<\beta<10<β<1. A spectral gap measures how quickly the slowest mode of a reversible dynamics relaxes. This all-observables statement is stronger than controlling only linear combinations of spins.

The cutoff manuscript goes further. With dn(t)=max⁡x∥Pt(x,⋅)−π∥TVd_n(t)=\max_x\|P_t(x,\cdot)-\pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}}dn​(t)=maxx​∥Pt​(x,⋅)−π∥TV​, it claims a deterministic rate λ(β)>0\lambda(\beta)>0λ(β)>0 and location

tn=log⁡n2λ(β).t_n=\frac{\log n}{2\lambda(\beta)}.tn​=2λ(β)logn​.

For every fixed 0<ε<10<\varepsilon<10<ε<1, it claims dn((1−ε)tn)→1d_n((1-\varepsilon)t_n)\to1dn​((1−ε)tn​)→1 and dn((1+ε)tn)→0d_n((1+\varepsilon)t_n)\to0dn​((1+ε)tn​)→0, in probability over disorder. This abrupt drop on the relative time scale is called cutoff. The paper does not identify the smaller cutoff window or give a simple closed formula for λ(β)\lambda(\beta)λ(β).

The critical power law

The critical slowing-down manuscript claims that, at any deterministic time tn=o(n2/3)t_n=o(n^{2/3})tn​=o(n2/3), almost all Gibbs-sampled fixed states remain more than 1/41/41/4 from equilibrium. Here o(n2/3)o(n^{2/3})o(n2/3) means tn/n2/3→0t_n/n^{2/3}\to0tn​/n2/3→0.

tmix=n2/3+o(1).t_{\mathrm{mix}}=n^{2/3+o(1)}.tmix​=n2/3+o(1).

Universal critical memory curves

The two October manuscripts examine more than mixing time. At equilibrium, define the mean spin autocorrelation

Cn,J(t)=1n∑i=1nEπJ,1[σi(0)σi(t)].C_{n,J}(t)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb E_{\pi_{J,1}}[\sigma_i(0)\sigma_i(t)].Cn,J​(t)=n1​i=1∑n​EπJ,1​​[σi​(0)σi​(t)].

The expectation averages the equilibrium initial state and the subsequent dynamics, keeping JJJ fixed. The stationary manuscript claims that n1/3Cn,J(sn2/3)n^{1/3}C_{n,J}(s n^{2/3})n1/3Cn,J​(sn2/3), for positive sss, converges in distribution as a function of time to a random limiting curve. Gaussian couplings and independent fair-sign couplings Jij=±1J_{ij}=\pm1Jij​=±1 give the same limiting law: this is universality. Individual disorder samples still have different curves.

The quench manuscript starts instead from independent fair spins, then runs the dynamics at β=1\beta=1β=1. A quench is this sudden change to the new temperature. The rescaled two-time correlation, with positive waiting time and lag both of order n2/3n^{2/3}n2/3, has a joint universal limit with the stationary curve. In that limit, increasing the waiting time recovers the stationary correlation. This is not a statement of universality for every coupling distribution.

5. Why the advance matters

If correct, the family turns the physical distinction between rapid equilibration, critical slowing, and persistent low-temperature memory into quantitative statements about a canonical random system. It also separates three questions that a single phrase such as “slow mixing” can hide: worst-case sampling, relaxation from typical equilibrium configurations, and the decay of particular observables.

For sampling algorithms, fast mixing gives a way to generate approximately equilibrium-distributed configurations by local updates. Low-temperature typical-start bounds, however, presuppose an equilibrium sample; they do not themselves provide an efficient method for obtaining one.

Importance: 4/5, provisional. The claimed transition across all fixed temperatures, the sharp critical exponent, and the universality results would constitute a major advance in probability and statistical mechanics. The score measures that mathematical significance if the claims hold. It is separate from verification, and will be calibrated against other families as the series grows.

6. The main idea

Independent spins explain the logarithm

At β=0\beta=0β=0, every redraw is a fair coin. Starting from all plus spins, the expected total magnetization at time ttt is ne−tne^{-t}ne−t: a spin retains its initial bias only if its clock has not rung. Equilibrium fluctuations of the total magnetization have scale n\sqrt nn​. Equating these scales gives

ne−t≈n⟹t≈12log⁡n.ne^{-t}\approx\sqrt n \quad\Longrightarrow\quad t\approx\tfrac12\log n.ne−t≈n​⟹t≈21​logn.

This calculation motivates the cutoff location but is not a full total-variation proof. With interactions, the cutoff manuscript tracks the sensitivity of evolved observables to individual spins. Upper bounds show that all distinguishability decays; lower bounds construct an observable whose initial bias remains detectable. The difficulty is making these estimates uniform over starting configurations.

Collective modes explain critical slowing

Think of diagonalizing a matrix to identify directions that respond weakly to a restoring force. Near criticality, certain collective spin directions become unusually soft. The manuscripts relate those directions to the largest eigenvalues of the normalized interaction matrix, with entries Jij/nJ_{ij}/\sqrt nJij​/n​, whose edge fluctuations occur on the scale n−2/3n^{-2/3}n−2/3.

This suggests a reciprocal time scale n2/3n^{2/3}n2/3, but Ising spins cannot simply be replaced by independent linear modes. The critical proofs compare them with auxiliary spherical spins—real vectors constrained to a sphere—and use progressively revealed noisy observations to control remaining fluctuations. They then transfer functional inequalities back to the discrete dynamics. The stationary and quench papers additionally identify the effective limiting evolution and compare Gaussian with sign disorder. These are proof strategies, not deductions from the eigenvalue scale alone.

Overlap detects barriers

The overlap between configurations is

R(σ,τ)=1n∑iσiτi.R(\sigma,\tau)=\frac1n\sum_i\sigma_i\tau_i.R(σ,τ)=n1​i∑​σi​τi​.

It equals one for identical configurations and minus one for opposite configurations. A single spin update changes it by at most 2/n2/n2/n, so local dynamics cannot jump across a macroscopic interval of overlaps.

The lower-bound paper uses constrained equilibrium comparisons and sequences of overlap crossings to obstruct rapid loss of memory for typical states. For the upper bound, the companion paper constructs probabilistic connections between sets of appreciable equilibrium mass, rules out excessively small flow between them, and converts that flow into typical-start mixing. Rare slow states can remain outside this conclusion. The two arguments explain why the lower and upper bounds concern a large fraction of states rather than a maximum over all states.

That document does not list formalizations of the sharp critical upper bound, the low-temperature typical-start upper bound, or the two universality manuscripts. This digest reports the published scope; no Lean build or independent audit of the complete proofs was performed for it.

The temperature is fixed before the system size tends to infinity. These statements do not automatically extend to nonzero fields, temperatures approaching criticality with nnn, all disorder laws, or finite-size numerical predictions. Universality here concerns the specified autocorrelation processes, not every mixing statement in the family. The repository README also explicitly allows for corrections to unformalized results.
